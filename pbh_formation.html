01 / HORIZON ENTRY

R_H = c/H = 2ct
M_H = (4π/3)ρ R_H³ = c³t/G

In a spatially flat radiation-dominated universe, a ∝ t¹ᐟ² and H = 1/(2t). Initially, the patch expands with the background while the Hubble radius grows faster. Horizon entry occurs when the physical patch radius equals c/H. The Hubble radius is not a black hole event horizon.

02 / PRESSURE VS GRAVITY

p = ρc²/3,   c_s = c/√3
δ_H > δ_c → collapse branch

Pressure can disperse a weak perturbation. A sufficiently large overdensity may form a PBH. The threshold δ_c depends on the perturbation profile and its definition. The default 0.450 is illustrative; the Analytic δ_c preset uses the Harada–Yoo–Kohri estimate ≈ 0.4135.

03 / NEAR-CRITICAL COLLAPSE

M_PBH = K M_H (δ_H − δ_c)γ
γ = 0.36

Near the threshold, small changes in δ_H can substantially change the mass. We use K = 1 as an illustrative normalization, not a universal calibration. Mass is shown only for 0 < δ_H − δ_c ≤ 0.1, a chosen demonstration window rather than a guaranteed validity range.

Scientific conventions, limitations and references

Calculated quantities: the branch selected by the chosen threshold, radiation-background scales, an indicative near-critical mass, its Schwarzschild radius and the initial analytic profile.

Schematic elements: tracer trajectories, turnaround, dispersal and the time when the black disk appears. The Einstein–Euler equations are not integrated, no trapped surface is detected and no event horizon is computed. The black disk has symbolic size and does not display the calculated r_s to scale. Photons, gravitational lensing and accretion disks are not simulated.

Comparison mode: the two patches have δ_H = δ_c ± 0.02, the same t_H and a shared spatial scale. Metric cards and plots always refer to the main δ_H slider.

The analytic estimate is δ_c = [3(1+w)/(5+3w)] sin²[π√w/(1+3w)], with w = 1/3. Full profile dependence is not included here.

The illustrative compensated profile is δ(x) = e δ_H (1 − 2x²/3) exp(−x²), with δ̄(<x) = e δ_H exp(−x²). Here e is Euler’s number. The integrated excess mass tends to zero as x → ∞. Relating this drawing to δ_H is a visualization convention, not a complete set of nonlinear GR initial data.

Export, controls and normalization K

Changing the initial conditions resets the animation. Use the time slider to inspect any stage while paused. Space toggles playback when no control has focus. CSV exports contain the initial profile and parameters, not numerical collapse data.

K depends on the profile family. Adjusting it does not calibrate this model against numerical GR.
